# Long manuals and parallel product reconstruction

## Bounded evidence packets

Start with a visual page map, then divide at natural subassembly or operation
boundaries. Inspect every supplied page, but do not load the entire manual into
each worker. Give each packet the designated finished view and its relevant pages.
Return compact records keyed by physical PDF page, step and column: part identity,
joint, installed orientation, dimensions, uncertainty and dependencies. Reopen
specific evidence when resolving a conflict rather than rereading every packet.

Parallel columns may show different parts, handed versions or alternatives.
Reconcile them against the final configuration and inventory before summing or
discarding them. Distinguish dimensioned envelopes, accessory footprints, local
offsets and adjustment ranges. The user's finished-product image chooses the
configuration when a generic cover or optional branch differs. Keep labels,
cartoons, tools and translucent instructional overlays out of the part inventory.

## One interface contract before separate CAD modules

Record units, final axes, overall envelope and common datums centrally. For each
module boundary record mating planes, stock thickness, offsets, hole axes, receiver
wall scope, hardware owner and motion owner. Assign one writer to each module;
unresolved shared dimensions belong to the coordinating owner, not independent
worker guesses. A module should emit stable unique part names and a short evidence
and assumptions record rather than duplicate the whole assembly specification.

When a dimension changes, update its dependent interfaces and placements together.
Do not scale an entire subassembly to fit: stock gauge, holes, hardware and accessory
dimensions may need to stay fixed. Recheck only the changed interfaces and bounds.

## Assembly feasibility can challenge a plausible silhouette

Check screw heads and insertion/tool-access envelopes as well as shaft centerlines.
A screw can align with its receiver yet be trapped behind an adjacent panel. First
revisit the inferred width, datum, receiving face and assembly order; do not invent
access holes or arbitrarily resize parts just to make the model fit. Record any
revised hidden dimension as inferred and seek corroboration in another view.

At integration, verify unique identities, envelope and one representative of each
distinct interface. Use isolated coupons for new shell or joinery construction.
Do not repeat expensive whole-product audits when unchanged modules already pass.

## Preserve animation-ready meaning

Part identity, inventory grouping and motion ownership are different relationships.
A hinge assembly can contain a cabinet-fixed plate, door-fixed cup and articulating
links; parenting the whole inventory group to the door is wrong. Preserve original
assembled matrices, explicit fixed/moving members and withdrawal-axis hints.
Keep screws and connectors individually identifiable, while factory-welded parts
normally move together. Label estimated pivots and reveal paths as illustrative,
not mechanically solved kinematics.
